BEFORE RECORDING

Open the local Streamlit app. In PowerShell, from the EBAY-research workspace:

    Set-Location 'project two'
    py -3.11 -m streamlit run python/app.py

If Streamlit is installed in a separate project environment, use that
environment's Python instead. Open the local URL printed by Streamlit.
Keep an app tab on the default settings and open docs/decision-memo.md in a
second tab or editor. Scroll the memo to “Base-case comparison” and
“Stress results and reversal triggers.”

Rehearse the liquidity check once yourself. In the app, select VT under
“Funds unavailable within five days.” Confirm that Equal weight shows 75.0%
five-day liquidity, and that the warning reports a 1.34% return ceiling against
a 5.50% hurdle. Clear VT before recording. Read the attestation and first-person
review statements below only after you have reviewed and can stand behind them.

1. OPENING AND AI DISCLOSURE

SHOW — The local app at the top of the page, with “Retirement Portfolio Review”
and the default scenario controls visible.

SAY
“This is my Project Two retirement portfolio review. I used OpenAI Codex to
organize the lab evidence, help build the Streamlit app, and write verification
checks. I reviewed the analysis and take responsibility for the decision.
This project is for education and does not constitute investment advice.”

2. HOW THE SYSTEM CHANGED

SHOW — Scroll to “Portfolio comparison.” Point to Equal weight, Inverse
volatility, Constrained, and 60/40 benchmark. Scroll just enough to show the
rule verdicts below the comparison.

SAY
“My starting point was the Lab 15 prototype, which compared an edited set of
inputs with the loaded inputs. I expanded it into a committee review. The app
compares four portfolios and shows which rules each one passes. In the base
case, the constrained mix clears the five point five percent net return hurdle
with an estimated five point seven four percent return.”

SHOW — Point to the Constrained row’s Net annual value. Then scroll to
“Required stress runs” and pause on the rows marked Stop.

SAY
“The base case looked promising. Seeing the required stresses changed my
decision. Several of them leave no mix that meets every rule. That led me to
recommend zero dollars in live funding and to keep using the system for paper
monitoring.”

3. ONE AI RESULT I CHECKED MYSELF

SHOW — Return to “Portfolio comparison.” In the sidebar, open “Funds
unavailable within five days,” select VT, and let the app refresh.

SAY
“Here is one AI-assisted result I checked myself. An early AI-written test
expected that making VT unavailable would rule out every mix under the rules
other than return. I checked that against the mandate. It requires ninety-five
percent liquidity within five days, which allows up to five percent in VT in
this scenario.”

SHOW — Point to Equal weight’s 25.0% VT weight and 75.0% Five-day liquidity.
If a column is off screen, scroll the comparison table horizontally.

SAY
“Equal weight puts twenty-five percent in VT, so only seventy-five percent
counts toward five-day liquidity. That fails the liquidity rule.”

SHOW — Point to “STOP: no feasible mix” in the Constrained row, then to the
warning below the tables. Keep the 5.50% hurdle and 1.34% ceiling visible if
possible.

SAY
“The warning says the highest estimated net return under the other rules is
one point three four percent. Some mixes still satisfy those rules, but even
the best of them falls short of the five point five percent hurdle. That
explains why the app stops and why the original test needed to change.”

4. CLOSE

SHOW — Bring the app and its stop warning back into view. End the recording
after the final sentence, then clear VT in the sidebar.

SAY
“The biggest change in my thinking was to ask what would make me stop funding
the strategy. The app now shows the reason for a stop, so I can check the
decision before committing capital.”
